\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Where the networks did well.} With smooth and compatible data the method is accurate: $4\times10^{-5}$
on the two-dimensional vortex and 0.27\% of a converged field on the $\Rey=1000$ cavity. The inverse problem, in
which sparse velocities and unknown coefficients have to be reconciled with the equations, is where the approach
is clearly useful: two coefficients to better than 0.1\% and a pressure field that was never measured to 2.4\%,
at a cost of half a GPU-hour. The trained networks are continuous, differentiable functions that can be queried
at millions of points per second and sampled at any resolution; the drag integrals, divergence maps and the
vortex-tube animation of this study use exactly that.

\paragraph{Where a classical solver wins.} For a single forward solve the comparison is not close: our simple
finite-difference code produced the cavity reference on a CPU in ten minutes to an hour, while the best network
needed 1.6~GPU-hours and other configurations were 20--28\% wrong. We did not run a production solver, so no
measured comparison with one is claimed.

\paragraph{What the failures have in common.} The largest errors of the study share one pattern: the network
found a smooth function with small residuals everywhere that nevertheless violates a conservation law. Exact lid
data incompatible with continuity left a divergence floor at the cavity corners; an impulsive inflow and, even after
a ramp, a long channel let a velocity--pressure network lose most of the mass; and in three dimensions the energy
left through the momentum residual instead of through viscosity. Pointwise residual losses do not control these
integral quantities, and conservation checks (flow rate through cross-sections, the two dissipation estimates)
diagnosed the failures faster than the residuals did. Exact divergence through a stream function restored most of
the mass balance. Gradient-norm weighting amplified the problem in every case: it gives each loss term the same
share of the update, so terms that are satisfied or easy received weights of $465$ (cavity lid),
$2.4\times10^5$ (outflow) and $6\times10^9$ (initial $w$), while the hard terms were de-weighted. Terms with an
identically zero target should at least be excluded from it.

\paragraph{References and limitations.} The Ghia $v$ table is about 2\% from grid-converged solutions, so a 2\%
gate on it cannot be met even by an exact solution: gates should be stated against references that solve the same
problem well beyond the required accuracy. Limitations: one seed per configuration; reduced budgets for the
cylinder, three-dimensional and inverse runs, and no cylinder ablation; timings 1.3--1.5 times pessimistic for runs
on a shared GPU; no $64^4$ separable grid (16~GB); a digitised three-dimensional reference; no OpenFOAM or FEniCSx
ground truth.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} The representation-side components (Fourier features, gated networks, exact periodicity,
stream functions) worked as intended where the data were compatible. The constraint- and weighting-side components
need care: exact boundary data fail when the data are incompatible with the equations, and gradient-norm balancing
can reinstate that failure through the weights. Two of six gates were passed (A and the inverse problem) and the
parametric model missed its gate by 0.11 points; the cavity gate cannot be met by any exact solution, and the
cylinder and three-dimensional gates are out of reach of these budgets. Code, reference solver, logs and evaluation
files for every number in this paper are in the accompanying repository.
