\subsection{A: two-dimensional Taylor--Green vortex}
The full configuration (row G, stream function, 50k Adam steps) reaches a velocity error of $4.05\times10^{-5}$
and a pressure error of $2.59\times10^{-4}$ with zero divergence, passing the gate by a factor of 2.5 (with one
gauge constant for the whole space--time domain the same model would show $3.3\times10^{-2}$). In the short
ablation (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) the rows end between 0.9\% and 3.2\%; at 3k steps the plain network (A) is
the most accurate, among the others the error falls steadily from C to G, and the stream-function rows (D--G) are
divergence free by construction while the VP rows keep $|\nabla\cdot u|$ of $10^{-2}$--$10^{-1}$.

\subsection{B: lid-driven cavity}
\label{sec:res-cavity}

\caption{Cavity at $\Rey=1000$. Left: centreline velocities of three configurations against the FD solution of
the same regularised-lid problem (grey) and Ghia et al.\ (circles). Right: $|\nabla\cdot u|$ with the exact lid
(row F) and with soft boundary data (row C); the exact lid leaves $|\nabla\cdot u|\sim10$ at the top corners and an
unconverged interior.}
\label{fig:cavity}
\end{figure*}

No configuration passes the 2\% gate on $v$, and the finite-difference reference shows that none can. As the grid
is refined the unit-lid solution converges to the spectral primary vortex~\cite{botella1998} and moves away from
Ghia's $v$ table (1.7\% at $128^2$, 2.1\% at $256^2$, 2.8\% at $512^2$); the converged regularised-lid solution is
2.29\% from it, because Ghia's $v$ values next to the right wall are about 0.01 smaller in magnitude than the
converged fields. Row C (soft boundary data, fixed weights) is 0.25\% from that field at the end of Adam and
0.27\% after L-BFGS, below the 0.56\% by which our $256^2$ and $512^2$ solutions differ (Fig.~\ref{fig:cavity}).

The ablation attributes the failures. With the exact lid, rows D and F end 26--28\% from the reference whether
the weights are fixed or adapted: continuity does not settle below $\sim10^{-2}$, whereas row C reaches
$2\times10^{-8}$, and the divergence map shows the corner floor predicted in Section~\ref{sec:method}. The soft
lid brings row D to 1.9\%. With gradient-norm weighting the soft lid fails again (row F, 21\%): the lid term has
small gradients, its weight rises to about 465 and makes the lid effectively exact, while the continuity weight
falls to 0.2. L-BFGS on one fixed batch of 8192 points is also unsafe here: on the soft-lid row D it lowered that
loss from $2.6\times10^{-3}$ to $2.3\times10^{-6}$ and tripled the field error (1.9\% to 6.7\%).

\subsection{C: flow past a cylinder}
Four attempts with reduced budgets failed for reasons that have little to do with budget. With the VP network
and the impulsive start of the benchmark, drag stayed between 0.3 and 0.5, a tenth of the developed value, and the flow
rate through the channel fell to 34\% of the inflow at $x=0.6$ and 2\% at the outlet after 0.5~s: the inflow
advanced as a slow front. A fluid at rest given the full inflow must accelerate everywhere at once, which a
representation smooth in time cannot do, so it trades continuity for smoothness. Ramping the inflow as
$\sin^2(\pi t/2)$ over the first second (the periodic state is unaffected) cut the continuity loss 20--80 fold,
but still only 12\% of the inflow crossed $x=0.6$: with continuity as one loss term among eight, a small
divergence everywhere adds up to a large loss of mass along a channel 22 diameters long. A stream-function
network with exact divergence and soft wall data kept 75--77\% of the flow rate and reached $C_{D,\max}=1.54$ in
two windows (1~s, still inside the ramp), against 3.22 for the developed flow; its remaining loss is leakage
through the soft wall conditions. No run reached the shedding regime, so $St$ is undefined and the gate failed.

\subsection{D: Taylor--Green vortex at $\Rey=1600$}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/tgv3d_energy.pdf}
\caption{Benchmark D: kinetic energy, the two dissipation estimates of the network against the digitised
reference~\cite{debonis2013}, and the spectrum at $t=9$.}
\label{fig:tgv3d}
\end{figure}
The separable network fits the initial state ($E_k(0)=0.1246$, exact $1/8$) and then decays smoothly
(Fig.~\ref{fig:tgv3d}). $-\dd E_k/\dd t$ is largest at $t=0$ (0.0292) and $2\nu\zeta$ never exceeds
$4.9\times10^{-4}$, errors of 127\% and 96\% against the reference peak. The two estimates should agree and
differ by a factor of about 60: the energy leaves through the momentum residual (mean square
$2.5$--$7\times10^{-4}$), not through viscosity. The vertical velocity stays near zero, so there is no vortex
stretching, and the spectrum at $t=9$ falls as $k^{-3.5}$ rather than $k^{-5/3}$. Gradient-norm weighting gave
the initial-condition term for $w$, whose target is identically zero, a weight of $6\times10^9$. The $64^4$ grid
needed one 11~GB buffer and did not fit, so this run used $32^4$ points and a quarter of the planned steps.

\subsection{Inverse problem and parametric model}
From 5000 velocity samples the wake network recovered $\lambda_1=0.99997$ and $\lambda_2=0.010006$ (errors 0.004\%
and 0.065\%) and the pressure, never observed, to 2.44\% (velocity 0.54\% and 2.69\%) on 20 snapshots, with a
quarter of the planned budget. The DeepONet first diverged twice: sampling $\Rey$ up to 1000 from the start, and
again with the curriculum, in both cases as soon as the warm-up reached the peak rate of $10^{-3}$ (10.5\% and
7.8\% at $\Rey=100$ after 5k steps, after which both diverged; the first ended at 70--87\%). At $3\times10^{-4}$ with
clipping, training was stable: after the 200k-step curriculum the error against the FD field was 2.3\% at $\Rey=100$,
14.0\% at the unseen $\Rey=400$ and 40\% at $\Rey=1000$; a 150k-step continuation over the full range brought these
to 2.0\%, 11.1\% and 33.5\%, at which
point the decayed learning rate stopped further progress. Two more legs, with a slower decay and then at a
constant rate of $1.5\times10^{-5}$, reached 10.27\% and then a plateau between 9.82\% and 10.11\%. The final model
is 10.11\% from the FD field at the unseen $\Rey=400$ (2.2\% at 100, 30.6\% at 1000) after 600k steps, three times
the planned budget: the gate is missed by 0.11 points. The high end of the range is the weak part of the model, as
for the single-$\Rey$ cavity, which needed 140k steps at $\Rey=1000$ alone. Once trained, a new $\Rey$ costs one
forward pass (about $10^7$ points per second).

Table~\ref{tab:cost} lists the cost of the main runs. A trained network answers $3.9\times10^6$ to
$1.2\times10^7$ point queries per second, and the separable network $2\times10^8$ on a grid. Training is expensive: the best cavity network took

minutes. All runs of this study together, including the failed and interrupted ones, used 30.0 GPU hours.
