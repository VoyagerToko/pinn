\section{Benchmarks, reference data and protocol}
\label{sec:setup}

Every forward problem is trained on the equations alone; reference data are used only to grade the result.
Only the inverse problem puts measured data into the loss. All runs use the same random seed. Relative $L^2$
errors $\|\hat q-q\|_2/\|q\|_2$ are evaluated on grids or point sets that are not the collocation points.

\subsection{A: two-dimensional Taylor--Green vortex}
On the periodic square $[0,2\pi]^2$ with $\Rey=100$ and $t\in[0,1]$ the decaying vortex
$u=-\cos x\sin y\,e^{-2t/\Rey}$, $v=\sin x\cos y\,e^{-2t/\Rey}$,
$p=-\tfrac14(\cos2x+\cos2y)\,e^{-4t/\Rey}$~\cite{taylor1937} is an exact solution. It checks the residual
implementation: the test suite verifies that the exact fields give residuals below $10^{-10}$ in double
precision before any network is trained. The network (stream-function form in the full configuration) sees
the initial condition and the equations; errors are measured on $5\times64\times64$ space--time points. Since
the pressure of an unsteady incompressible flow is determined only up to a function of time, the predicted
pressure is shifted to the mean of the exact pressure in each time slice before its error is computed. Gate:
relative velocity error below $10^{-4}$.

\subsection{B: lid-driven cavity}
The unit square has no-slip walls and a lid moving in $+x$ with the regularised profile
$u_{\mathrm{lid}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:method} ($r=50$), which removes the velocity discontinuity of the
classical problem at the upper corners. Training follows the $\Rey=100\to400\to1000$ curriculum. The gate is
the relative error of the centreline profiles $u(0.5,y)$ and $v(x,0.5)$ at $\Rey=1000$ against the tables of
Ghia et al.~\cite{ghia1982}, below 2\% for both, evaluated at the tabulated stations without the wall and lid
points. We transcribed the tables by hand and validated the transcription against the reference fields
distributed with JAX-PI~\cite{jaxpi}: under the same metric the two agree to within 5\% at $\Rey=100$, 400 and
1000 (at $\Rey=1000$: 2.5\% for $u$, 1.8\% for $v$).

Because Ghia et al.\ and the JAX-PI fields use a unit lid, neither is a solution of the problem the PINN
actually solves. We therefore wrote an independent finite-difference solver for the regularised problem:
stream function and vorticity on a uniform grid, second-order central differences, the wall vorticity from
Thom's formula~\cite{thom1933}, the Poisson equation solved exactly for the five-point Laplacian with a
type-I sine transform, and second-order explicit pseudo-time stepping until
$\max|\partial_t\omega|<10^{-7}$. At $\Rey=1000$ the unit-lid solutions on $128^2$, $256^2$ and $512^2$ grids give
minimum stream-function values $-0.11547$, $-0.11807$ and $-0.11872$, which extrapolate (second order) to
$-0.11894$, the spectral value of Botella and Peyret~\cite{botella1998} ($-0.1189366$); Ghia et al.\ report
$-0.11793$. The regularised-lid solution on the $512^2$ grid is our field reference for $\Rey=1000$
(the $256^2$ and $512^2$ fields differ by 0.56\%), and $256^2$ solutions serve at $\Rey=100$ and 400.

\subsection{C: DFG 2D-2 flow past a cylinder}
The channel, cylinder, viscosity $\nu=10^{-3}$ and parabolic inflow with maximum 1.5 (mean $\bar U=1$) follow
Sch\"afer and Turek~\cite{schaefer1996}; with the diameter $D=0.1$ as length scale $\Rey=100$ and the flow
sheds vortices periodically. At the outlet the do-nothing condition $\Rey^{-1}\partial_xu-p=0$,
$\partial_xv=0$ is imposed as a loss. Drag and lift coefficients are computed from the surface integral of
the viscous and pressure tractions on 256 quadrature points, with the velocity gradient on the wall from the
network by automatic differentiation; the Strouhal number $St=fD/\bar U$ uses the dominant frequency of a
Hann-windowed Fourier transform of $C_L(t)$ over the second half of the record. Gates: $St$ within 3\% of 0.30
and $C_{D,\max}$ within 5\% of 3.23. The paper by Sch\"afer and Turek was not retrievable at the address in
our reference list; its reference intervals ($C_{D,\max}\in[3.22,3.24]$, $C_{L,\max}\in[0.99,1.01]$,
$St\in[0.295,0.305]$) were taken from the FEATFLOW benchmark pages~\cite{featflow}, together with the
FEATFLOW drag and lift time series used for comparison. The value $St\approx0.16$--0.17 often quoted for
$\Rey=100$ holds for an unconfined cylinder; in this confined channel the reference value is 0.30. The
FEATFLOW series (finest level, file \texttt{bdforces\_q2\_lv6\_dt3}) is periodic from its first sample, and
itself gives $St=0.3002$, $C_{D,\max}=3.2201$ and $C_{L,\max}=0.986$; because the PINN starts from rest, curves
are compared after aligning the phase of the last lift maximum.

\subsection{D: three-dimensional Taylor--Green vortex}
The triply periodic box $[0,2\pi]^3$ at $\Rey=1600$ starts from $u=\sin x\cos y\cos z$,
$v=-\cos x\sin y\cos z$, $w=0$~\cite{brachet1983}; the flow transitions to small-scale turbulence around $t\approx8$--9
and decays until $t=20$. We compute the kinetic energy $E_k(t)$ and enstrophy $\zeta(t)$ as grid averages
on $32^3$ points, the dissipation rate $\varepsilon=-\dd E_k/\dd t$ and its incompressible counterpart
$2\nu\zeta$, and the shell-summed energy spectrum on $64^3$ points. The gate is the dissipation peak within
5\% of the reference. The case descriptions of the high-order CFD workshops~\cite{wang2013hiocfd} contain no
numerical curves, so we digitised the reference-solution curve of Figure~4(a) in DeBonis~\cite{debonis2013}
from a 600~dpi rendering of the report, following the black line pixel by pixel. The digitised curve peaks at
$\varepsilon=0.01282$ at $t=9.00$; as a check, its value at $t=0$ is $4.4\times10^{-4}$ against the analytic
$3/(4\Rey)=4.69\times10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Inverse problem and parametric surrogate}
The cylinder-wake data set of~\cite{raissi2019pinn} (spectral-element simulation at $\Rey=100$, 5000 points
by 200 snapshots in a window behind an unconfined cylinder) provides $(u,v)$ at 5000 randomly chosen
space--time samples. The network (stream-function form) is trained on these velocities plus the residual
\eqref{eq:rm} with unknown $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$, both initialised at zero; the targets are $\lambda_1=1$
and $\lambda_2=\nu=0.01$. The pressure is never shown to the network; its error is measured on every tenth
snapshot after aligning the mean per snapshot.

The PI-DeepONet is trained over $\Rey\in[100,1000]$ drawn log-uniformly, four values per step, except that the
band $[300,530]$ is never sampled; $\Rey=400$ is therefore a query the model has not seen. Gate: velocity
error at $\Rey=400$ below 10\% against the reference field.

\subsection{Ablation protocol and budgets}
Table~\ref{tab:rows} lists the configurations. Rows A--G are run on Benchmarks A and B with the same seed
and collocation budget within a benchmark; row H (the separable network) exists only for D. For the steady
cavity, causal weighting is inactive, so rows D and E coincide. The planned budgets were
200k Adam steps per cylinder time window, 300k for D and full budgets for every ablation row, about
150--160 GPU hours in total; the work had to be completed within one day of GPU time, so the budgets
actually used are reduced as stated in Table~\ref{tab:budget}. All runs used one RTX~5070~Ti (16~GB) under
WSL2; for most of the runs reported here the card was shared with an unrelated video-analytics workload
using about 70\% of it, which slowed our steps by a factor of about 1.3--1.5.
