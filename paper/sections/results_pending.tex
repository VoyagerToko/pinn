\subsection{C: flow past a cylinder}
\label{sec:res-cylinder}
\input{sections/table_cylinder}
The first attempt followed the planned time marching with sixteen windows but only 4000 Adam steps per window.
After two windows (1~s of flow) it was stopped: the drag coefficient was 0.32 at $t=0.25$~s, 0.36 at 0.5~s and
0.45 at 0.75~s, an order of magnitude below the value of about 3.2 of the developed flow, and the front--rear
pressure difference was 0.19--0.29 instead of about 2.5. The continuity and momentum losses were still at
$2\times10^{-2}$ and $6\times10^{-3}$, the causal weights had not converged (minimum weight 0.92--0.94 with the
tolerance still at $\varepsilon=0.1$), and gradient-norm balancing had raised the weight of the outflow
condition on $v$ to 7000--9000 because its gradient is tiny. The drag integral itself is checked against
analytic fields in the test suite, so the low drag reflects a boundary layer and near wake that the network had
not yet resolved. Continuing would have propagated this state through fourteen more windows and could not
produce the shedding needed for a Strouhal number, so the same GPU time was spent on four windows of 12k steps.

Three times as many steps did not change the picture: after 12k steps in the first window $C_D$ was still 0.31 at
$t=0.25$~s and 0.32 at 0.5~s, the continuity loss stayed at $2$--$3\times10^{-2}$, and the outflow weight had grown
to $2.4\times10^5$. The flow rate through cross-sections of that model identifies the cause. The exact inflow
constraint delivers $0.41~\mathrm{m^2/s}$ at $x=0$ from $t=0$ on, but at $t=0.5$~s the network carries
$0.24$ at $x=0.1$, $0.14$ at $x=0.6$, $0.03$ at $x=1.2$ and $0.008$ at the outlet: the inflow advances into the
channel as a slow front. For an incompressible fluid at rest that is suddenly given the full inflow, the whole
channel must accelerate at once; a representation that is smooth in time cannot follow this jump and gives up
continuity instead, which is the temporal counterpart of the corner problem of the cavity. The runs below
ramp the inflow over the first second (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

With the ramp the optimisation behaves as intended: after 12k steps of the first window the continuity loss
is $3\times10^{-4}$--$10^{-3}$ (20--80 times lower than with the impulsive start), the momentum loss
$2\times10^{-5}$--$2\times10^{-4}$, and the causal tolerance has been raised to $\varepsilon=10$. Mass is still not
conserved, however. At the end of the window only 12\% of the inlet flow rate crosses $x=0.6$ and 2\% leaves
through the outlet, and the pressure drop along the channel is about $0.18$~Pa where accelerating the channel flow
requires several pascals. The network balances momentum locally by keeping the downstream fluid nearly at rest
and accepts a divergence of a few hundredths everywhere: small at each collocation point, but large once it
is integrated over a channel 22 diameters long. With the velocity--pressure output, continuity is one loss term
among eight, and on this long domain its per-point residual is simply not a strong enough constraint on the
global flow rate. The drag coefficient accordingly stays near 0.1. Table~\ref{tab:cylinder} lists the attempts;
all were stopped early, and none reached the periodic regime, so the Strouhal number is undefined and the gate
is failed.
