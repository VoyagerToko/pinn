\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Governing equations and residuals}
All problems are solved in non-dimensional form. With a reference velocity $U$, length $L$ and kinematic
viscosity $\nu$, the Reynolds number is $\Rey=UL/\nu$, and velocity $\bu=(u,v[,w])$, pressure $p$ (divided by
the constant density) and time are scaled by $U$, $U^2$ and $L/U$. For a network output that provides $\bu$
and $p$ at a point $z=(t,\bx)$ we define the momentum and continuity residuals
\begin{align}
\mathbf{r}_m(z) &= \partial_t\bu + \lambda_1(\bu\cdot\nabla)\bu + \nabla p - \lambda_2\,\nabla^2\bu, \label{eq:rm}\\
r_c(z) &= \nabla\cdot\bu, \label{eq:rc}
\end{align}
with $\lambda_1=1$ and $\lambda_2=1/\Rey$ in every forward problem. In the inverse problem the two
coefficients are free parameters learnt together with the network. Steady problems drop $\partial_t\bu$.

Three output parametrisations are used. In the \emph{velocity--pressure} (VP) form the network returns
$(\bu,p)$ directly and continuity is a loss term. In two dimensions the \emph{stream-function} form returns
$(\psi,p)$ and sets $u=\partial_y\psi$, $v=-\partial_x\psi$, so that $\nabla\cdot\bu=\partial_{xy}\psi-
\partial_{yx}\psi$ vanishes identically; the momentum residual then contains third derivatives of $\psi$. In
three dimensions the \emph{vector-potential} form returns $(\mathbf{A},p)$ with $\bu=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$,
again divergence-free by construction. Since $\mathbf{A}$ is defined only up to a gradient, a weak Coulomb gauge
$\lambda_g\langle(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A})^2\rangle$ with $\lambda_g=10^{-3}$ is added to the loss.

\paragraph{Derivatives.} For a point-wise network $f:\mathbb{R}^d\to\mathbb{R}^k$ every derivative in
\eqref{eq:rm}--\eqref{eq:rc} is obtained as a directional derivative in forward mode: the Jacobian--vector
product $f'(z)\,e_i$ with the unit vector $e_i$ gives $\partial_i f$ for all outputs at once, and nesting
two such products gives $\partial_i f$ and $\partial_{ii} f$ together. A VP residual in $d_s$ spatial
dimensions therefore costs one first-order and $d_s$ second-order directional derivatives; the stream-function
and vector-potential residuals apply the same operator to a velocity that is itself a directional
derivative. No Jacobian or Hessian tensor is formed. An earlier version of the code that built full
Jacobian and Hessian tensors needed 63~GB for a stream-function batch that the directional form evaluates in
a few gigabytes. Reverse-mode differentiation of the loss with respect to the parameters is then applied on
top. To bound memory, the residual is evaluated over a batch in chunks of 2048 points that are
rematerialised in the backward pass, so peak memory scales with the chunk and not with the batch.

\subsection{Network architectures}
\paragraph{Input embeddings.} Periodic coordinates enter through $(\cos\omega x,\sin\omega x)$ with
$\omega=2\pi/P$, which makes the representation exactly periodic with period $P$. Non-periodic inputs,
normalised to $O(1)$, are passed through random Fourier features~\cite{tancik2020fourier}
\begin{equation}
\gamma(z)=\big[\cos(Bz),\ \sin(Bz)\big],\qquad B_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2),
\end{equation}
with 128 frequencies (256 features). The scale $\sigma$ is a per-benchmark choice: $\sigma=1$ for the
Taylor--Green and cylinder problems, $\sigma=10$ for the cavity, $\sigma=2$ for the wake inverse problem.

\paragraph{Modified MLP and weight factorisation.} The main point-wise architecture is the gated network
of~\cite{wang2021gradient}. From the embedding $h_0=\gamma(z)$ two encodings
$U=\tanh(W_Uh_0+b_U)$ and $V=\tanh(W_Vh_0+b_V)$ are formed once, and each hidden layer computes
\begin{equation}
g_\ell=\tanh(W_\ell h_{\ell-1}+b_\ell),\qquad h_\ell=g_\ell\odot U+(1-g_\ell)\odot V,
\end{equation}
followed by a linear output layer. We use six hidden layers of width 256 unless stated otherwise (eight of
width 128 for the inverse problem; GELU instead of tanh for the cylinder). Every weight matrix is
factorised as $W=V\,\mathrm{diag}(e^{s})$ with $s\sim\mathcal{N}(1,0.1^2)$ at initialisation, and $V$ and $s$ are
trained jointly~\cite{wang2022rwf}; the factorisation is folded back into a plain matrix for inference.
Ablation rows without the modified MLP use a plain tanh MLP of the same depth without factorisation.

\paragraph{Exact boundary conditions.} Dirichlet data can be built into the ansatz~\cite{lagaris1998,
sukumar2022distance},
\begin{equation}
\hat\bu(z)=g(z)+\varphi(z)\,N_\theta(z),
\label{eq:hard}
\end{equation}
where $g$ is any smooth function carrying the boundary values, $\varphi$ vanishes on the Dirichlet boundary
and is positive inside, and $N_\theta$ is the network. For the unit cavity $\varphi=x(1-x)y(1-y)$ and
$g=(u_{\mathrm{lid}}(x)\,y^{8},0)$; the high power keeps $g$ in a thin layer below the lid so that $N_\theta$
does not have to cancel a shear across the whole cavity. For the DFG channel $[0,2.2]\times[0,0.41]$ with a
cylinder of radius $r=0.05$ at $(0.2,0.2)$ we use
\begin{equation}
\varphi=\tanh\!\Big(\frac{x}{2r}\Big)\,\frac{4y(H-y)}{H^2}\,\tanh\!\Big(\frac{d}{r^2}\Big),\quad
d=(x-0.2)^2+(y-0.2)^2-r^2,
\end{equation}
which vanishes on the inlet, both walls and the cylinder but stays bounded in $[0,1]$ over the channel, and
$g_u=u_{\mathrm{in}}(y)\,d/(d+x)$, $g_v=0$. Only the outflow condition remains a loss term. The product of the
three polynomial factors without saturation, a common textbook choice, grows by four orders of magnitude
between the cylinder and the outlet, which conditions the network poorly.

\paragraph{A compatibility obstruction at the cavity corners.} The regularised lid profile used here,
$u_{\mathrm{lid}}(x)=1-\cosh(r(x-\tfrac12))/\cosh(\tfrac r2)$ with $r=50$, vanishes at $x=0$ and $x=1$ but has
slope $u'_{\mathrm{lid}}(1)=-r\tanh(r/2)\approx-50$. If \eqref{eq:hard} imposes both the lid and the no-slip
side walls exactly, then along $x=1$ the velocity vanishes identically, so $\partial_yv=0$ at the corner, while
along $y=1$ continuity demands $\partial_yv=-\partial_xu=-u'_{\mathrm{lid}}$. No continuously differentiable
field satisfies both, so $\nabla\cdot\hat\bu=O(r)$ in a corner patch of width $O(1/r)$ whatever the network
does: a mean-square continuity floor of order $10^{-2}$. We therefore also use a variant in which the
distance function for $u$ is $x(1-x)y$ (zero on the three fixed walls only) while $v$ keeps the full
$\varphi$; the tangential lid velocity is then enforced by a loss term $L_{\mathrm{lid}}$ on the lid. We call
this the \emph{soft-lid} constraint; Section~\ref{sec:res-cavity} tests both.

\paragraph{Separable PINN.} For the three-dimensional unsteady problem the network is separable in its four
inputs~\cite{cho2023spinn}: each output component is
\begin{equation}
f_o(t,x,y,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{R} f^{(t)}_{o,j}(t)\,f^{(x)}_{o,j}(x)\,f^{(y)}_{o,j}(y)\,f^{(z)}_{o,j}(z),
\end{equation}
where each factor is the output of a small network of one variable (four gated layers of width 64, rank
$R=128$). Periodic spatial axes are embedded with the harmonics $\cos kx,\sin kx$, $k=1,\dots,4$. On a tensor
grid of $n_t\times n_x\times n_y\times n_z$ points the network is evaluated $n_t+n_x+n_y+n_z$ times, and a
Jacobian--vector product with a tangent of ones along one axis returns the exact partial derivative along
that axis at every grid point, because each grid value depends on a single entry of each coordinate vector.

\paragraph{Physics-informed DeepONet.} For the parametric cavity the solution operator is represented
as~\cite{lu2021deeponet,wang2021pideeponet}
\begin{equation}
G_\theta(a)(\bx)=\sum_{k=1}^{p}b_k(a)\,\tau_k(\bx)+b_0,\qquad p=128,
\end{equation}
with the scaled parameter $a=(\log_{10}\Rey-2)/2$, a branch network $b$ (three tanh layers of width 256)
and a trunk $\tau$ that is a modified MLP with Fourier features (six layers of width 256), separately for
each of the three outputs. The physics loss is applied to $G_\theta(a)$ for a random set of Reynolds numbers
in every step; the boundary treatment is the same as for the single-$\Rey$ cavity.

\subsection{Loss function and weighting}
The objective is a weighted sum of mean-square terms,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\sum_i\lambda_i\,\mathcal{L}_i(\theta),
\end{equation}
where the terms are, depending on the problem, the momentum residual per component
($\mathcal{L}_{r_u},\mathcal{L}_{r_v},\dots$), the continuity residual (VP only), boundary and lid mismatches
(soft constraints only), initial-condition mismatches, the outflow residual, data mismatch (inverse problem),
the vector-potential gauge and a pressure anchor. In closed or periodic domains the pressure is fixed by
$\mathcal{L}_p=\big(\tfrac1N\sum_ip(z_i)\big)^2$, which enforces zero mean.

\paragraph{Gradient-norm balancing.} Following~\cite{wang2021gradient,wang2023expert}, every 1000 steps we
compute $\hat\lambda_i=\overline{G}/G_i$ with $G_i=\|\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_i\|_2$ and $\overline{G}$ the mean of
all $G_i$, and update $\lambda_i\leftarrow0.9\lambda_i+0.1\hat\lambda_i$. Terms that can be driven to exactly
zero (the pressure anchor and the gauge) are kept at weight one, since their gradient norm vanishes and the
update would diverge.

\paragraph{Causal weights.} For unsteady problems the residual points of a batch are sorted in time and
split into $M$ equal chunks with mean square residuals $\mathcal{L}_1,\dots,\mathcal{L}_M$. The residual
loss is replaced by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_r=\frac1M\sum_{k=1}^{M}w_k\mathcal{L}_k,\qquad w_k=\exp\Big(-\varepsilon\sum_{j<k}\mathcal{L}_j\Big),
\end{equation}
with the weights treated as constants in the gradient~\cite{wang2024causality}; with several residual
equations the element-wise minimum of their weights is used. Later chunks thus receive gradient only once
the earlier ones are resolved. The tolerance $\varepsilon$ is raised through $10^{-2},10^{-1},1,10,100$
whenever $\min_kw_k>0.99$ (checked every 1000 steps); $M=32$ for the cylinder and $M=16$ along the time axis of
the separable grid.

\paragraph{Residual-based adaptive sampling.} Every 1000 steps $10^5$ candidate points are drawn uniformly and
a pool of 65{,}536 collocation points is resampled with probability proportional to
$|r(z)|/\mathbb{E}|r|+1$~\cite{wu2023rad}; 20\% of the pool is kept uniform so that sampling cannot collapse
onto one region. Batches are then drawn from the pool.

\subsection{Training procedure}
Stage one is Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam} with a linear warm-up to a learning rate of $10^{-3}$ followed by
exponential decay by a factor 0.9 every 2000 steps, in single precision with the highest matrix-multiply
precision. Stage two is limited-memory BFGS~\cite{liu1989lbfgs} (history 20, zoom line search satisfying the
strong Wolfe conditions) on one fixed collocation batch, stopped after 50 iterations without relative
progress above $10^{-12}$ or at an iteration cap. The cavity is trained with a Reynolds-number curriculum
$\Rey=100\to400\to1000$ (20k, 40k and 140k steps at the full budget); the weights are carried over between
stages and the optimiser state, including warm-up and decay, is restarted. The cylinder is marched in
windows of $0.5$~s (five convective time units): window $n$ starts from the network of window $n-1$ and uses
its state at the end of the window as initial condition. Both the curriculum and the marching follow the
remedies proposed in~\cite{krishnapriyan2021failure}. The separable network uses a
fresh random tensor grid of $32^4$ points in every step.
