\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Finite-volume, finite-element and spectral solvers for the incompressible Navier--Stokes equations are fast and
well understood for a fixed geometry and fixed parameters. Physics-informed neural networks~\cite{raissi2019pinn}
take another route: a network represents velocity and pressure as smooth functions of space and time, the
residuals of the equations are evaluated at scattered points by automatic differentiation, and training drives
these residuals and the boundary and initial mismatches towards zero. No mesh is needed, derivatives of the
representation are exact, and measurements can enter the same objective, which suits flow reconstruction from
sparse data~\cite{raissi2020hfm} and surrogates queried at arbitrary points.

The optimisation behind this is often badly conditioned. Networks resolve smooth components first, the loss
terms have gradients of very different size, unsteady solutions can be learnt out of temporal order, and
convection-dominated problems collapse onto trivial
states~\cite{wang2021gradient,wang2022ntk,krishnapriyan2021failure}. Many remedies have been
proposed~\cite{tancik2020fourier,wang2022ntk,wang2024causality,wu2023rad,wang2022rwf,wang2024piratenets,jin2021nsfnets,
lagaris1998,sukumar2022distance,cho2023spinn,jagtap2020xpinn,lu2021deeponet,wang2021pideeponet,li2021fno} and
collected into practical advice~\cite{wang2023expert}, usually each shown on the problem that motivated it.

We test the combination in one code base, built in JAX~\cite{jax2018github} along the lines of
JAX-PI~\cite{jaxpi} and SPINN~\cite{spinncode}, on a ladder of problems with gates fixed before training: the
two-dimensional Taylor--Green vortex (exact solution), the lid-driven cavity against Ghia et
al.~\cite{ghia1982}, the DFG cylinder benchmark of Sch\"afer and Turek~\cite{schaefer1996}, the
three-dimensional Taylor--Green vortex at $\Rey=1600$, an inverse problem on the wake data
of~\cite{raissi2019pinn}, and a DeepONet over the cavity Reynolds number. Our contributions are:
(i) a memory-bounded implementation in which all residuals are built from forward-mode directional derivatives;
(ii) an independent finite-difference reference for the regularised-lid cavity, which shows that the 2\% Ghia gate
cannot be met even by an exact solution, and against which the best network is 0.27\% off;
(iii) a diagnosis of three ways in which the full recipe produces networks that break conservation: exact lid
data incompatible with continuity, an impulsive inflow, and gradient-norm weights of up to $6\times10^9$ on
terms that are already satisfied; (iv) results and costs for every run, including the failed gates and the
reduced budgets.
